Geometric Insight

When the top leans, gravity creates a twisting effect that acts sideways—not downward as you might expect.

  • The center of mass is offset horizontally by a distance \(d = h \sin\theta\) from the pivot.
  • Gravity pulls down with force Mg, creating a torque \(\tau = Mg h \sin\theta\) that points horizontally, perpendicular to the top's spin axis.
  • The spinning top has angular momentum L = I\(\omega\) directed along its tilted axis.

Because torque changes the direction of angular momentum, not its magnitude, the top's axis slowly rotates around the vertical instead of falling over. In a short time, the angular momentum vector sweeps out a small horizontal angle.

This sideways torque causes the angular momentum vector to rotate around the vertical axis. The faster the spin (larger \(L\)), the slower this rotation. The stronger the torque (larger \(\tau\)), the faster the rotation. The exact relationship is derived in the next step.
